# S1 generalized trust — public-source acquisition

## Neville (2012) pooled state estimates
Table 4 of Lukas Neville (2012), *Do Economic Equality and Generalized Trust Inhibit Academic Dishonesty?* reports state-level generalized trust estimates. The trust measure pools nationally representative surveys: six ANES waves (2000–2010), 1999 NYT Millennium Survey, 2000 Social Capital Benchmark Survey national sample, 2006 Social Capital Community Survey national sample, 2010 Pew Political Independents poll, and 2010 Pew Trust in Government survey. All use the standard generalized-trust item. Alaska and Hawaii are excluded in the published trust estimates; DC is included.

IMPORTANT: these are pooled 1999–2010 estimates, NOT year-2000 estimates. They are stored as an auxiliary calibration/validation source and must not be assigned to 2000.

## SCCBS 2000
The 2000 Social Capital Community Benchmark Survey has N=29,233 and the exact TRUST item: "Would you say that most people can be trusted or that you can't be too careful in dealing with people?" Public aggregate frequency: 14,502 trusted (49.6%), 12,612 can't be too careful (43.1%), 1,902 depends, 174 don't know, 43 refused. The design includes a national sample (N=3,003) plus 42 communities across 29 states. Roper documents the state FIPS field as restricted-use, so the public SCCBS file cannot by itself yield a verified 50-state 2000 panel.

Acquisition rule: restricted geocoded GSS/SCCBS fields are not used. Continue searching only public state-level sources for year-specific anchors.
