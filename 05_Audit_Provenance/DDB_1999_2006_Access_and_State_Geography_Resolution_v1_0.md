# DDB 1999–2006 Access and State-Geography Resolution v1.0
Date: 2026-09-24

## Finding 1 — post-1998 microdata existed and were used by researchers
Published work uses DDB Needham Life Style microdata after the public Putnam archive:
- 2002 survey: about 3,200 respondents; study states most questions remained the same as in 1995.
- 2003 survey: 5,000 mailed, 3,021 usable responses.
- 2004 survey: published research uses the DDB Life Style microdata.
- 2005: longitudinal academic analysis uses DDB through 2005.
- 2006 survey: 5,188 adult respondents; published research explicitly analyzes the 2006 DDB Needham Life Style dataset.

Therefore the Putnam public archive cutoff is not the survey cutoff.

## Finding 2 — access route was direct/proprietary, not a public repository
A published 1985–2005 analysis explicitly acknowledges Chris Callahan at DDB Needham for providing Life Style Survey data for 1999–2005.
Other literature describes the post-1998 LSS as proprietary while the 1975–1998 archive is freely available on Putnam's site.

Canonical implication:
Do not waste time treating the Putnam download page as the expected repository for 1999–2005. The documented route is direct DDB/researcher access or a lawful secondary archive if one can be located.

## Finding 3 — respondent state geography exists in post-1998 microdata
Research using the DDB Life Style Survey states that the data include state of residence and estimates models with state fixed effects / state-year policy variables.
Thus state geography is not merely inferred from Census region.

Canonical implication:
P11/P12/P14 can in principle be estimated at state level from post-1998 microdata, subject to sample-size, weights, wording, and access checks.
