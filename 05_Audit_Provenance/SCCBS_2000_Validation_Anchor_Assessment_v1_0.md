# SCCBS 2000 — Validation Anchor Assessment v1.0
Date: 2026-09-23

## Source
Social Capital Community Benchmark Survey (SCCBS), designed by the Saguaro Seminar / Kennedy School and conducted in 2000.

## Verified sample architecture
- National sample: approximately 3,003 respondents, continental U.S.
- Community component: approximately 26,700 additional respondents.
- 42 communities across 29 states reported by Roper.
- Most community samples are cities, counties, or multi-county regions rather than representative state samples.
- Published methodological use of SCCBS identifies four community samples corresponding to entire states: Delaware, Indiana, Montana, and New Hampshire.

## Geography restriction
Roper documentation lists state FIPS among geographically sensitive restricted-use fields.
Therefore public-use SCCBS must not be assumed to support unrestricted respondent-level 50-state estimation.

## Canonical role in this project
SCCBS 2000 is NOT a 50-state baseline panel.

Allowed roles:
1. NATIONAL 2000 measurement-validation anchor for social-capital constructs available in the national sample.
2. STATEWIDE validation anchor for Delaware, Indiana, Montana, and New Hampshire, subject to weights/design verification.
3. COMMUNITY-level construct-validation/sensitivity analysis for sampled communities.
4. Crosswalk source for comparing modern social-capital item wording/dimensions with Putnam-era constructs.

Disallowed:
- imputing unsampled states;
- treating community samples as state-representative;
- claiming 50-state 2000 coverage;
- using restricted state geography without lawful access;
- merging community estimates into the canonical 50-state panel as if sampling frames were equivalent.

## Measurement value
Roper describes SCCBS as measuring friendship/socializing, groups and associations, trust, and civic engagement. This makes it especially useful for validating the project's proposed dimensions:
- Associational-Civic;
- Informal Sociability;
- Trust-Normative;
- Civic-Political.

Exact item-by-item equivalence to P01-P14 must still be checked in the codebook before any item is called a strict Putnam equivalent.
