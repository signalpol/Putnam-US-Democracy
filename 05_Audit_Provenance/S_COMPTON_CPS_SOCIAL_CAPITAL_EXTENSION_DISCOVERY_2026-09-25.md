# Compton CPS Social Capital Extension — discovery note (2026-09-25)

## Major finding
Mallory Elise Compton, *The Political Economy of Unemployment Insurance* (Texas A&M PhD dissertation, 2016; chair Kenneth J. Meier) explicitly develops a new state-level social-capital measure in response to limitations of Hawes, Rocha & Meier (2013).

## Coverage and sources
The dissertation reports more than 3 million CPS responses used to generate state-year social-capital estimates over 2000–2013.

Table 3.1 source waves:
- CPS Volunteer Supplement: 2002–2009, 2011–2013
- CPS Civic Engagement Supplement: 2008–2011
- CPS Voting and Registration Supplement: 2000, 2002, 2004, 2006, 2008, 2010, 2012
- CPS Participation in the Arts: 2002, 2008, 2012

It aggregates responses to state-year behavioral indicators and, following Hawes, Rocha & Meier (2013), uses principal-component factor analysis. It distinguishes at least two factors: Charitable & Voluntary Social Capital and Civic & Political Social Capital.

## Critical methodological constraint for our project
Compton uses multiple imputation for structurally unavailable survey-wave observations before factor analysis. Our canonical Putnam project prohibits interpolation/imputation of missing values. Therefore:
1. preserve Compton's published/replication index as an external-research series;
2. do NOT silently merge imputed Compton values into the strict series;
3. prioritize acquisition of the underlying observed CPS state-year indicators and preserve unavailable years as NA.

## Significance
This confirms the user's hypothesis that later scholarship constructed longitudinal state-level social-capital data from public source data following Hawes-style factor analysis. It directly bridges our Hawes series into the 2010–2013 period at the source-data level, subject to strict no-imputation handling.
