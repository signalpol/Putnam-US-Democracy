# Closest Precedents and Core References — Putnam U.S. Democracy Project
Date: 2026-09-24
Status: canonical working reference set; bibliographic details web-verified where indicated.

## Purpose
This file isolates the literature most directly comparable to the project's central empirical hypothesis:
Economic Conditions Change -> Social Capital Weakening -> Trump-Era Political Transformation -> Sharper Democratic Deterioration.
Similarity does not mean any prior work tests this full sequence. The studies below supply the closest component designs, measurements, causal warnings, and subnational precedents.

## Tier A — Closest overall precedents
1. Bordandini, Paola, ed. (2025). *Civic Decline or Civic Shift? Social Capital in Italy*. Cham: Palgrave Macmillan. DOI: 10.1007/978-3-032-14753-0.
   - Thirty-year reassessment of Putnam using subnational Italian evidence; diachronic province-level measurement; explicitly asks decline vs transformation.
   - Closest overall methodological precedent.

2. Bordandini, Paola & Luca Bortolotti (2025). “A Geography of Social Capital in Italy 2008–2022.” In *Civic Decline or Civic Shift?*, pp. 95–115.
   - Province-level longitudinal social-capital index; economic/pandemic crisis context; social and political participation diverge.

3. Ferragina, Emanuele (2013). “The socio-economic determinants of social capital and the mediating effect of history: Making Democracy Work revisited.” *International Sociology* 54(1). DOI: 10.1177/0020715213481788.
   - 85 European regions; directly models socioeconomic determinants of social capital. Inequality, labor-market participation, and cohesion are central to the project's E -> S mechanism.

4. Rice, Tom M. & Alexander F. Sumberg (1997). “Civic Culture and Government Performance in the American States.” *Publius: The Journal of Federalism* 27(1):99–114. DOI: 10.1093/oxfordjournals.pubjof.a029899.
   - Directly extends Putnam's regional method to U.S. states; constructs civic-culture and government-performance indices.

5. Knack, Stephen (2002). “Social Capital and the Quality of Government: Evidence from the States.” *American Journal of Political Science* 46(4):772–785. DOI: 10.2307/3088433.
   - U.S. states; social capital and quality of government. Direct precedent for state-level S -> institutional-performance testing.

6. Paxton, Pamela (2002). “Social Capital and Democracy: An Interdependent Relationship.” *American Sociological Review* 67(2):254–277. DOI: 10.1177/000312240206700205.
   - Treats democracy and social capital as interdependent; central warning against assuming only S -> D and basis for reverse-path tests.

## Tier B — U.S. social-capital trajectory and economic determinants
7. Paxton, Pamela (1999). “Is Social Capital Declining in the United States? A Multiple Indicator Assessment.” *American Journal of Sociology* 105(1):88–127. DOI: 10.1086/210268.
   - Multiple-indicator U.S. trend study; no uniform decline across trust and associations. Core precedent for dimensionality and measurement-invariance tests.

8. Costa, Dora L. & Matthew E. Kahn (2003). “Understanding the American Decline in Social Capital, 1952–1998.” *Kyklos* 56(1):17–46. DOI: 10.1111/1467-6435.00208.
   - U.S. long-run decline mechanisms; income inequality helps explain decline in community social capital. Highly relevant to E -> S.

9. Alesina, Alberto & Eliana La Ferrara (2002). “Who Trusts Others?” *Journal of Public Economics* 85(2):207–234. DOI: 10.1016/S0047-2727(01)00084-6.
   - U.S. localities; economic disadvantage and income disparity are associated with lower trust. Micro-level support for economic/social conditions -> trust.

10. Rupasingha, Anil, Stephan J. Goetz & David Freshwater (2006). “The Production of Social Capital in US Counties.” *Journal of Socio-Economics* 35(1):83–101. DOI: 10.1016/j.socec.2005.11.001.
    - U.S. counties; explicitly models individual/community inputs into production of social capital using administrative and socioeconomic data.

## Tier C — Putnam causal tests and replications
11. McLaren, Lauren M. & Vanessa A. Baird (2006). “Of Time and Causality: A Simple Test of the Requirement of Social Capital in Making Democracy Work in Italy.” *Political Studies* 54(4). DOI: 10.1111/j.1467-9248.2006.00638.x.
    - Temporal-order challenge; suggests democratic institutions can foster social capital. Direct basis for D(t-1) -> S(t) robustness.

12. Barceló, Joan (2016). “Re-examining a modern classic: does Putnam's Making Democracy Work suffer from spuriousness?” *Modern Italy*.
    - Tests political-party confounding in Putnam's relationship. Basis for omitted-political-structure robustness.

13. Pierce, J. C., Nicholas P. Lovrich Jr. et al. (2016). “Social capital, institutional performance, and sustainability in Italy's regions: Still evidence of enduring historical effects?” *The Social Science Journal* 53(3):271–281. DOI: 10.1016/j.soscij.2016.06.001.
    - Links historic and contemporary social capital to contemporary institutional performance; mediation through contemporary social capital.

14. Tarrow, Sidney (1996). “Making Social Science Work Across Space and Time: A Critical Reflection on Robert Putnam’s Making Democracy Work.” *American Political Science Review* 90(2):389–397.
    - Core critique of historical/path-dependence inference and causal reconstruction.

15. Levi, Margaret (1996). “Social and Unsocial Capital: A Review Essay of Robert Putnam’s Making Democracy Work.” *Politics & Society* 24(1):45–55.
    - Institutions/state action and non-benign social capital; prevents treating social capital as automatically democracy-enhancing.

16. Boix, Carles & Daniel N. Posner (1998). “Social Capital: Explaining Its Origins and Effects on Government Performance.” *British Journal of Political Science* 28.
    - Mechanisms linking social capital and government performance; origins/endogeneity relevant to causal specification.

## How these map to the present paper
- E -> S: Ferragina; Costa & Kahn; Alesina & La Ferrara; Rupasingha et al.
- S -> D / government performance: Putnam; Rice & Sumberg; Knack; Pierce et al.
- D -> S / endogeneity: Paxton 2002; McLaren & Baird.
- Measurement of long-run S change: Paxton 1999; Bordandini/Bortolotti.
- Confounding/path dependence: Tarrow; Levi; Boix & Posner; Barceló.
- Subnational-to-national reassessment architecture: Putnam; Bordandini; Rice & Sumberg; Knack.

## Current research gap
None of the verified studies in this closest-precedent set is being treated as evidence that the full proposed U.S. sequence has already been established. The current project tests whether economic change precedes social-capital weakening and whether those pre-existing changes help account for a statistically sharper deterioration in U.S. democratic performance during the Trump-era political transformation. This remains a hypothesis, not a predetermined finding.

## Verification rule
Before manuscript citation, verify full bibliographic metadata and, where possible, full text. Abstract-only verification is insufficient for claims about methods or results beyond what the abstract supports.
