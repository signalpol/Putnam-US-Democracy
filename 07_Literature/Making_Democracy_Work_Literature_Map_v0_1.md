# Making Democracy Work — Literature Map v0.1
Date: 2026-09-23

## Purpose
Map research that directly tests, critiques, replicates, or extends Putnam, Leonardi & Nanetti (1993), with special attention to:
(1) social capital -> institutional/democratic performance;
(2) socioeconomic conditions -> social capital;
(3) socioeconomic conditions -> institutional/democratic performance;
(4) reverse causality and endogeneity;
(5) historical/path-dependence claims;
(6) measurement and generalizability.

## Foundational
- Putnam, Leonardi & Nanetti (1993), Making Democracy Work. Core claim: civic community/social capital is strongly associated with regional institutional performance; historical civic traditions are treated as persistent roots.
- Putnam et al., "Explaining Institutional Success: The Case of Italian Regional Government." Earlier empirical formulation: socioeconomic development, participant/sociable political culture, and social stability jointly explain a large share of regional institutional performance. IMPORTANT for the present project because socioeconomic development was not literally absent from the authors' earlier empirical ecology.

## Causal mechanism / underspecification
- Boix & Posner (1996), "Making Social Capital Work." Argues that micro-linkages from associations/social cooperation to good government are underspecified; association types differ; political conflict is underdeveloped; questions external validity and discusses economic performance.
- Levi (1996), "Social and Unsocial Capital." Major critical review emphasizing institutions, state action, and the problem of assuming socially beneficial effects of social capital.
- Tarrow (1996), "Making Social Science Work Across Space and Time." Critiques the historical reconstruction/path-dependence inference and insufficient structural grounding when extending a study of regional institutional reform into a centuries-long explanation.

## Temporal ordering / reverse causality
- McLaren & Baird (2006), "Of Time and Causality." Earlier Civic Culture survey evidence does not support social capital as a necessary prior condition for North-South institutional development; suggests democratic institutions may foster social capital more rapidly than Putnam's long historical account implies.
Implication: present U.S. project must test lagged and reverse pathways rather than assume Social Capital -> Democracy only.

## Socioeconomic alternative / direct relevance to current project
- Ferragina (2013), "The socio-economic determinants of social capital and the mediating effect of history: Making Democracy Work revisited." Tests 85 European regions. Finds medieval history does not explain southern Italy's social-capital deficit in the proposed way; unequal income distribution, low labor-market participation, and weak national cohesion are central socioeconomic determinants, with history mediating their effects.
DIRECT DESIGN IMPLICATION: inequality, labor-market participation/insecurity, and material economic conditions belong in the U.S. model as determinants of social capital, not merely controls.
- Goldberg (1996), "Thinking about how Democracy Works." Argues Putnam dismisses standard economic explanations too quickly; directly relevant to the project's political-economy critique.
- Kenworthy (1997), "Civic Engagement, Social Capital, and Economic Cooperation." Argues Putnam overstates social capital's contribution to cooperation/economic success; institutional incentives matter and Italian findings may not generalize.
- Schneider, Plümper & Baumann (2000), "Bringing Putnam to the European Regions." Embeds political culture/social capital in a neoclassical regional growth framework to test economic-growth relevance beyond Italy.
- Beugelsdijk & van Schaik (2005), "Social capital and growth in European regions." 54 European regions; associational activity is positively related to regional growth, suggesting some generalizability of Putnam's economic linkage.
- de Blasio & Nuzzo (2005/2006; later J Regional Science 2010), "The legacy of history for economic development: The case of Putnam's social capital." Uses historical instruments and finds economic effects of social capital on productivity, entrepreneurship, and female labor-force participation.
Implication: economic conditions may be cause, consequence, confounder, or mediator; models must distinguish these roles.

## Spuriousness / political structure
- Barceló (2016), "Re-examining a modern classic: does Putnam's Making Democracy Work suffer from spuriousness?" Reassesses whether the Italian Communist Party confounds Putnam's empirical relationship.
Implication: omitted political structure can generate spurious civic-performance associations; U.S. models need political controls/robustness.

## Later replication / persistence
- Social Science Journal (2016), "Social capital, institutional performance, and sustainability in Italy's regions: Still evidence of enduring historical effects?" Finds historic and contemporary social capital correlated and related to contemporary institutional performance/sustainability, with historical effects appearing to operate through contemporary social capital.
- Carradore (2018), synthetic-indicator application to Putnam-style Italian social-capital indicators; confirms persistent North-South civicness pattern using alternative index construction.

## Thirty-year reassessment
- Bordandini et al. (2026), Civic Decline or Civic Shift? Mapping Social Capital Thirty Years After Making Democracy Work. Reassesses Italian civic culture and participation after three decades and explicitly asks whether observed change is decline or transformation in forms of participation.
Implication: current U.S. project should distinguish civic decline from civic shift and test measurement sensitivity to changing forms of participation.

## Current project's literature-derived model
Do not assume one-way causality.
Test:
E_t -> S_t
S_t -> D_t
E_t -> D_t
E_t x S_t -> D_t
D_(t-1) -> S_t (reverse pathway robustness)
and compare lagged specifications.

E = economic conditions (income/output, inequality, labor-market participation/insecurity, structural change)
S = strict Putnam social capital plus separately labeled modern extension
D = democratic/institutional performance

## Key contribution gap
The literature contains:
- Putnam-style S -> institutional performance;
- critiques of historical causality and measurement;
- evidence for socioeconomic determinants of S;
- evidence for S -> economic performance;
- tests beyond Italy.

The intended U.S. contribution is to integrate these strands in a 2000-2023 50-state longitudinal design focused on the long-run trajectory of U.S. democratic performance, while explicitly modeling economic conditions as determinants of social capital and as independent/joint predictors of democracy.
